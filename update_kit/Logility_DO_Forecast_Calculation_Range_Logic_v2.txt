## 1. What the Forecast Calculation Range is

The Forecast Calculation Range enables you to specify the range of periods when forecasts are
generated by the **Calculate Forecasts program (FCP)**.

For future forecast periods **outside** this range, the following forecast values are set to zero:

- Forced System Forecast
- Unforced System Forecast
- **Resultant Forecast**
- ADS1 Forecast, ADS2 Forecast, ADS3 Forecast

Historical periods are ignored. The As of Date (AOD) is a historical period, so the AOD period is
ignored.

### Prerequisite and integration caveat

- This feature **requires the Demand Optimization (DO) module**.
- Vendor note: if you are using Causal Forecasting, Demand Sensing, or Price and Promotion Analysis
  (PPA), you should check with Customer Support on the integration.

---

## 2. Behaviour when forcing (Force Forecasts, FIF)

The enhanced interactive and batch **Force Forecasts (FIF)** program respects the Forecast
Calculation Range.

- Periods **inside** the begin/end dates are processed based on existing forcing rules.
- Periods **outside** the begin/end dates are processed the same way as an **inhibited forecast**
  (Forecast Model Type = "Inhibited") when the Forecast Calculation Range Indicator is Fixed (F) or
  Annual (A).

Vendor example. You are forcing from Level 5 to Level 2 and a **Level 3 record** has a Forecast
Calculation Range:

- When inside the range, the Level 3 record is processed normally. Other records are processed normally.
- When outside the range, the Level 3 record is treated like inhibited. Other records are processed normally.

> This is the mechanism that produces the customer-level effect we describe operationally. The range
> itself is defined over **periods**, but it is held **on a record at a level of the forecast
> hierarchy**. Set it on a partner-level or Level 3 record and the practical result is that that
> record stops receiving forecast, which is what our User Manual calls "the customer no longer
> receives baseline disaggregation".

## 4. Annual Forecast Calculation Range

Annual indicates the range **repeats each year**. It is defined by a specified Begin Period and End
Period for each year in the forecast horizon, and the pattern repeats for each calendar year after
the AOD.

Rules:

- The Period must be a value from 0 to less than or equal to the number of periods per year
  (12 for monthly, 52 for weekly).
- Begin Period = **0** means the range begins on the **first future period** (the period after the AOD).
- End Period = **0** means the range ends in the **last period of the year**.
- Historical periods are ignored. The AOD period is historical, so it is ignored.

### Example A1: Annual, set begin and set end (March 03 through September 09), AOD April 2020

- January 2020 through April 2020 are historical, so ignored.
- May 2020 through September 2020 display the forecast values.
- October 2020 through December 2020 are outside the range, so zero.
- The March to September pattern repeats for each calendar year after AOD.

### Example A2: Annual, set begin and set end wrapping the year end (September 09 through March 03), AOD April 2020

- January 2020 through April 2020 are historical, so ignored.
- May 2020 through August 2020 are outside the range, so zero.
- September 2020 through March 2021 display the forecast values.
- April 2021 through August 2021 are outside the range, so zero.
- The September to March pattern repeats for each calendar year after AOD.

### Example A3: Annual, set begin and no end date (begins September, ends last period of year), AOD April 2020

- January through April are historical, so ignored.
- May through August are future periods before the begin period, so zero.
- September 2020 through December 2020 display the forecast values.
- The pattern where January through August are zero repeats for each calendar year after AOD.

### Example A4: Annual, no begin and set end date (begins first future period May, ends September 09), AOD April 2020

- January through April are historical, so ignored.
- May 2020 through September 2020 display the forecast values.
- October 2020 through December 2020 are outside the range, so zero.
- The pattern where October through December are zero repeats for each calendar year after AOD.

## 5. Fixed Forecast Calculation Range

For a fixed range you specify the exact period and year when the range begins and the exact period
and year when it ends.

Rules:

- The begin date must be less than or equal to the end date.
- The Year must be between 1950 and 2100.
- The Period must be a value from 0 to less than or equal to the number of periods per year
  (12 for monthly, 52 for weekly).
- Begin Period and Begin Year = **0** means the range begins on the **first future period**
  (the period after the AOD).
- End Period and End Year = **0** means the range goes to the **end of the future horizon**.

### Example F1: Fixed, set begin and set end (March 2020 through September 2020), AOD April 2020

- January 2020 through April 2020 are historical, so ignored. Note that March 2020 and April 2020
  were inside the specified range, but they are historical periods so they are ignored anyway.
- May 2020 through September 2020 display the forecast values.
- October 2020 through the end of the future horizon are outside the range, so zero.

### Example F2: Fixed, no begin and set end date (first future period May 2020 through September 2020), AOD April 2020

- January 2020 through April 2020 are historical, so ignored.
- May 2020 through September 2020 display the forecast values.
- October 2020 through the end of the future horizon are outside the range, so zero.

### Example F3: Fixed, set begin and no end date (September 2020 to end of future horizon), AOD April 2020

- January 2020 through April 2020 are historical, so ignored.
- May 2020 through August 2020 are future periods before the begin period, so zero.
- September 2020 through the end of the future horizon display forecast values.

## 7. What this means for HERO

**This section is Hasbro interpretation, not vendor text.** Written 21 August 2026, pending
confirmation from Jarred Bultema.

1. **The range zeroes values, it does not remove records.** The vendor wording is "the following
   forecast values are set to zero". So a SKU whose range excludes the cycle periods does not
   disappear, it comes through in zeros. HERO's own rule is that any planning SKU with a forecast
   record for the fiscal year is selectable, "even all zeros". Taken together: the Forecast
   Calculation Range is **not** what decides whether a SKU is available for enrichment in a HERO
   template. It decides whether the numbers arrive non-zero.

   **And the zeroing is conditional, which this page does not say.** It happens through the Calculate
   Forecasts program, which writes into a period only where the Resultant Forecast Override Indicator
   is `N` or `H`. Where the indicator is `M`, Hasbro's "Manual", the quantity and the indicator are
   **left as they were** and the out-of-range value survives. Hasbro uses only `M` and `H` today, so
   in practice: `M` preserves, `H` loses. This is Canonical Fact 106, and it is documented by the
   vendor on the Forecast Override Indicators page rather than on this one.
2. **The customer effect is a consequence of hierarchy level, not of the definition.** The range is
   defined over periods. It produces a per-customer outcome only because it is held on a record at a
   given level of the forecast hierarchy. Our internal shorthand ("adjust the forecasting range so
   this customer stops receiving disaggregation") is a correct description of the outcome, but it is
   not the definition, and training material should say both.
3. **The vendor's zeroing list does not include the UA arrays, and this is now closed.** The list
   covers Forced System Forecast, Unforced System Forecast, Resultant Forecast, and ADS1 to ADS3.
   UA1 to UA6, where HERO writes, are absent from it. **Closed 21 August 2026 by fact 88:** the range
   constrains what the Calculate Forecasts program produces, and the User Adjustment arrays are not
   statistically calculated, they are built bottom up from deliberate adjustments. There is nothing in
   them for the range to zero. Fact 94 closes the same question from the other direction for ADS2.
4. **Annual versus Fixed matters for seasonal ranges.** An Annual range repeats every year and can
   wrap the year end (Example A2). If Hasbro loads ranges as Annual, a product that only sells in a
   season will show zeros for the off-season in every future year, which is correct behaviour and
   should not be raised as a defect during the pilot.
5. **AOD periods inside the range are still ignored** (Example F1). Setting a begin date in the past
   buys nothing.

6. **The range constrains the force down, not the roll up.** Section 2 above says the Force Forecasts
   program respects the range, and section 1 says the Calculate Forecasts program is what zeroes. The
   **Summing program** is a third thing and it is not mentioned on this page: it brings the sum of the
   children up to the parent, operating only on Resultant Forecasts, and nothing here constrains it. So
   a value already sitting at Level 1 survives and rolls up, which is why a Level 3 number can end up
   not reconciling with the range that supposedly governs it. This is Canonical Fact 107, documented on
   the Forecast Override Indicators page.

7. **A range is a continuous period, not a year bucket** (Canonical Fact 112). One begin date, one end
   date, and every period outside them is out of range whatever calendar year it falls in. "We only use
   the range for 2027" describes when the mechanism was adopted, not how far it reaches.
